GEO PARETO PICKS — 7 moves covering 80–90% of AI search ranking
For: AI agency serving local businesses

THE META-PRINCIPLE
AI models are extreme skimmers. They read the first 100 words, scan headers, and check trust signals — in that order. Optimize exactly that path.

#1 — Write direct answer blocks at the top
Impact: Highest of all 7
What this is in simple words:
When someone asks an AI chatbot a question, the model grabs the clearest, most self-contained paragraph it can find — usually from the very top of a page. If your answer isn't right at the start, you don't get cited. Think of it like a newspaper headline: the most important thing comes first, not buried in paragraph 5.
Examples for your agency site:

On your homepage, the very first paragraph (before "who we are" or "our story") should read something like: "We help restaurants, salons, and retail shops in Bengaluru get more repeat customers using AI voice agents, automated review follow-ups, and loyalty programs — set up in under a week, no tech knowledge needed." That's the answer block. It answers "what do you do and for whom" in 2 sentences.
On your "AI Voice Agents" service page, open with: "An AI voice agent answers your customer calls 24/7, books appointments, handles FAQs, and follows up with missed callers — so your staff focuses on in-person service instead of phones." Not with "In today's competitive landscape…"
On your "Review Follow-up Automation" page, start with: "We set up automated WhatsApp and SMS follow-ups that ask happy customers to leave a Google review within 2 hours of their visit — most clients see a 3x review increase in the first month." Specific, direct, zero fluff.


#2 — Use question-based H2/H3 headers + FAQ sections
Impact: Very High
What this is in simple words:
AI models match a user's question to the heading text on your page before reading the content below it. If a heading literally mirrors what someone searched, the model treats that section as the relevant answer. Think of headers as labels on filing cabinet drawers — the AI opens the drawer whose label matches the question. An FAQ block at the bottom gets harvested almost verbatim by Perplexity, Bing Copilot, and ChatGPT Browse.
Examples for your agency site:

Instead of a section called "Our Services", break it into headers like: "How does an AI voice agent help a restaurant?" / "What is automated review follow-up and why does it matter?" / "How does a loyalty points program increase repeat visits?" — each one mirrors exactly how a local business owner would ask a question.
Add a FAQ section on your homepage with questions like: "How long does it take to set up an AI system for my cafe?" / "Do I need a developer to run these tools?" / "What's the difference between a loyalty program and a review follow-up system?" — write the answers in 3–4 plain sentences each.
On your landing page service pages, use an H3 like "Who is this for?" followed by a crisp answer: "Best for restaurants, salons, gyms, and retail shops with 10–200 customers per day who want to increase repeat visits without hiring more staff." Perplexity will pull that section word for word when someone asks "who needs AI automation for local businesses."


#3 — Add explicit author + source credibility signals
Impact: High
What this is in simple words:
AI models distrust anonymous pages. They look for signals that a real, qualified human wrote this — a name, a credential, a date, a cited source. It's the same reason you trust a doctor's advice more when you know their name and specialisation. Without these signals, even great content gets ranked below a mediocre page that has them.
Examples for your agency site:

Add a byline under every service page and blog post: "Written by [Your Name], AI automation consultant — 2 years building AI systems for Bengaluru F&B and retail businesses. Last updated: May 2026." That one line adds name, expertise, and freshness signal.
On your case study pages, cite specific results with attribution: "After implementing our review follow-up sequence, Brew & Bite Cafe (Koramangala) went from 14 to 67 Google reviews in 6 weeks — a 4.8x increase." Named business, named location, specific number, specific timeframe. Models love this format.
Add a short "About this agency" section in your footer with: "[Agency name] is a Bengaluru-based AI consulting firm specialising in voice agents, CRM automation, and loyalty programs for local businesses. Founded [year]. [X] clients served." This is the equivalent of Wikipedia's sourcing — it gives models a trust anchor.


#4 — Target semantic intent clusters, not just keywords
Impact: High
What this is in simple words:
Old SEO was about repeating a keyword like "AI for restaurants" as many times as possible. AI models don't work that way — they understand meaning. They want one page that covers an entire topic from every angle: the main answer, the follow-up questions, the objections, the comparisons. A thin 300-word page will lose to a thorough 1,500-word page even if the thin one has the exact keyword. Think of it like asking a friend vs. googling — you want the friend who gives you the full picture, not just one sentence.
Examples for your agency site:

Instead of separate thin pages for "AI voice agent" and "automated calls for restaurants," write one comprehensive page called "AI phone automation for restaurants and cafes" that covers: what it is, how it works, what happens after setup, what it costs, common objections ("will it sound robotic?"), and a real example. One deep page beats five shallow ones.
Write a cluster page titled "How local businesses in Bengaluru use AI to get more repeat customers" that covers loyalty programs, review follow-ups, WhatsApp automation, and voice agents all in one place — with each covered in 200–300 words. Perplexity will cite this page for at least 4–5 different question types.
For your ICP-specific landing pages (e.g. for salons), cover the full intent cluster: "What AI tools work for salons?" + "How does a salon loyalty program work?" + "Can a salon automate WhatsApp follow-ups?" + "What does AI consulting cost for a small salon?" — all on one page, in separate sections. This signals to models that your page fully answers the topic.


#5 — Implement structured data (Schema.org JSON-LD)
Impact: Medium-High
What this is in simple words:
Behind the scenes of every webpage, you can add a block of code (JSON-LD) that tells machines — including AI crawlers — exactly what your page is about in a structured format they can read without guessing. It's like adding a label on a box instead of making the delivery person read the whole box to figure out what's inside. FAQPage schema is especially powerful — it gets pulled directly into Google AI Overviews and Bing Copilot answers.
Examples for your agency site:

Add FAQPage schema to your homepage and service pages. Each FAQ you've written in human text (#2 above) should also exist as JSON-LD in the page's head tag. This makes Bing Copilot and Google AI pull your FAQ answers directly into their response panels, with your site credited.
Add LocalBusiness schema to your agency's homepage with your city (Bengaluru), service categories (AI consulting, marketing automation), and contact details. This helps any model that's localising results ("AI agency near me" or "AI consulting Bengaluru") find and trust your listing.
Add HowTo schema to your process/how-it-works page: "Step 1: Discovery call — Step 2: Custom automation audit — Step 3: Setup and integration — Step 4: Go live in 7 days." Perplexity and Google AI display HowTo schema as visual step cards, which is premium placement you get for free.


#6 — Get indexed in sources these models trust
Impact: Medium-High
What this is in simple words:
AI models don't just read your website — they also read Reddit, GitHub, Quora, YouTube descriptions, niche publications, and review sites. If you only exist on your own site, you're invisible in 60% of their source pool. The goal is to have your name, your content, or a mention of your agency appear in the places these models already trust and crawl. Think of it as word-of-mouth, but for AI systems.
Examples for your agency site:

Post a genuinely helpful Reddit thread in r/bangalore or r/IndianGaming: "Here's what 3 Bengaluru restaurant owners told me after we set up AI follow-up systems for them — data inside." Link to your case study page. Perplexity cites Reddit heavily. One good thread drives AI citations for months.
Publish a detailed GitHub README for any open-source template or workflow you've built (even a simple n8n automation template for review follow-ups). GitHub is one of the most trusted sources for every AI model. A repo titled "WhatsApp review follow-up automation for local businesses — India" will get cited anytime someone asks Perplexity about this topic.
Get your agency mentioned in at least one niche publication — a Bengaluru startup blog, YourStory, or even a guest post on a marketing automation site. One inbound mention from a trusted domain is worth more than 20 self-published pages for AI citation purposes.


#7 — Create an llms.txt + allow AI crawlers explicitly
Impact: Medium (but future-proof)
What this is in simple words:
There's an emerging standard called llms.txt — a simple text file you put on your website that tells AI crawlers which pages matter most, what your site is about, and how to read it. It's like a sitemap, but specifically for AI models instead of Google. Separately, many sites accidentally block AI crawlers in their robots.txt file without realising it — fixing that alone can restore your visibility in Perplexity and ChatGPT Browse overnight.
Examples for your agency site:

Create a file at yourdomain.com/llms.txt with plain-English descriptions of your key pages: "Homepage: AI automation agency for local businesses in Bengaluru. Services: AI voice agents, loyalty programs, review automation, landing page optimisation. Case studies: [link]. FAQ: [link]." Takes 20 minutes to write, keeps paying off as more models adopt the standard.
Check your robots.txt right now for lines that say "Disallow" against GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or Bingbot. If any of those bots are blocked, remove the block. This is the #1 silent killer of GEO visibility — site owners block AI crawlers by accident with generic "block all bots" rules copied from old templates.
On your most important pages (homepage, top service page, top case study), add an HTML meta tag: <meta name="robots" content="index, follow"> and ensure your page isn't behind any login, redirect chain, or JavaScript-only render that blocks crawlers from reading the content. If a bot can't read it, no model will cite it.
